Abstract

Cross-domain policy adaptation seeks to reuse source-domain knowledge under dynamics shifts with limited target-domain interaction. Model-based methods address this problem by adapting source-pretrained world models, but their apparent sample-efficiency advantage may partly arise from value transfer: model-based methods often retain source-trained value functions, while model-free baselines learn critics from scratch. When we give model-free baselines the same source-trained critic, they initially adapt faster than their Dyna-style counterparts. Further analysis shows that dynamics ensembles and prior-guided data collection jointly improve Dyna-style adaptation, allowing it to outperform the model-free baseline on average. Guided by these findings, we propose DomainADEPT, which improves model-based planning with a dynamics ensemble that increases model accuracy and with “tethered” fine-tuning of the proposal policy that limits policy drift during data collection. Across four contact-rich manipulation tasks and one locomotion task, DomainADEPT achieves the highest average final performance among the evaluated methods under matched target-domain interaction budgets, improving average success by 25 percentage points over the strongest baseline. In sim-to-real experiments on two manipulation tasks, DomainADEPT reaches 90–100% success after 50 real-world episodes, compared with 50–65% for SimDist.

Is it the world model, or the transferred value?

Latent model-based methods keep the source-trained value function while they adapt the dynamics, whereas model-free baselines usually train their critics from scratch. Once IBRL starts from the same source critic, it adapts faster than Dyna-IBRL. Dyna-IBRL only pulls ahead when it has both a dynamics ensemble and a margin that makes data collection prefer the prior policy.

What limits planning?

Planning with the world model adapts quickly at first and then plateaus. We look at the value, the dynamics and the proposal policy one at a time. With the target simulator as an oracle model, the frozen source value already plans well, so dynamics error sets the ceiling. Every value-update schedule we tried hurts. Fine-tuning the proposal on target demonstrations helps, and tethering it to the source policy helps more.

DomainADEPT

Domain Adaptation with Dynamics Ensembles and Policy Tethering

Like SimDist, we transfer a source-pretrained latent world model and plan with MPPI, keeping the value head frozen during adaptation. DomainADEPT changes two things.

1Plan-time dynamics ensemble

K dynamics heads are pretrained in the source domain from independent initializations and bootstrapped data, then fine-tuned on target transitions. The planner rolls out the mean prediction of the heads instead of any single head:

p̄(z, a) = (1/K) Σi p̂i(z, a)

Averaging the heads reduces model error.

2Tethered proposal fine-tuning

The proposal policy clones the target demonstrations while being penalized for drifting from the source policy on source states:

L(η) = E𝒟t‖πη(s) − a‖2 + λ E𝒟s D(πη ‖ πs)

This keeps the planner searching where the frozen dynamics and value heads are reliable.

Cross-domain adaptation in simulation

Four contact-rich MimicGen manipulation tasks and one locomotion task, each with a dynamics shift between source and target. All methods get 20 target demonstrations and the same target interaction budget. DomainADEPT adapts fastest and reaches about 70% average success, 25 points above the strongest baseline.

Both components matter

Sim-to-real

The source domain is a MuJoCo replica of the real cell; the target is a Franka Research 3 with a Robotiq 2F-85 gripper, observed through a global and a wrist camera. Each adaptation round adds 10 real episodes and is evaluated over 20 trials. After 50 real episodes DomainADEPT succeeds in 20/20 trials on Threading and 18/20 on Square, while SimDist reaches 10/20 and 13/20.

Each video shows the first 10 of the 20 evaluation trials, before and after adaptation; the caption keeps a running success count.
